paper topic: (by MIT)
Your Brain on ChatGPT: Accumulation of Cognitive Debt when Using an AI Assistant for Essay Writing Task
the mean idea:
The study divided participants into three groups and do research.
The group are:
•	LLM Group: Used ChatGPT exclusively for information and writing assistance.
•	Search Engine Group: Used Google Search (excluding AI-augmented results).
•	Brain-only Group: Relied solely on their own knowledge without external tools.
the key findings:
•	The Brain-only group showed the strongest and most widespread neural connectivity, particularly in the Alpha and Theta bands associated with internal attention, memory retrieval, and creative ideation.
•	The LLM group exhibited the weakest connectivity, reflecting significant "cognitive offloading," where the brain shifts the burden of planning and thinking to the AI.
•	Cognitive Debt: In Session 4, participants who switched from LLM to Brain-only still showed weak neural connectivity, suggesting that long-term reliance on AI may make it harder for the brain to enter a state of deep thinking even when the tool is removed.
•	Homogenization: Essays from the LLM group were highly similar in terms of Named Entity Recognition (NER) and thematic structure, lacking uniqueness.
•	Memory Decay: The LLM group's ability to accurately recall their own essay content minutes after writing was significantly lower than the other groups (only 16.7% could vaguely recall content, and none were fully accurate).
•	The LLM group felt the lowest sense of ownership, with many participants reporting feelings of guilt or viewing the work as AI-generated rather than their own.

Privacy and security concerns are a challenge in education because AI systems often require access to large amounts of data to function effectively.
